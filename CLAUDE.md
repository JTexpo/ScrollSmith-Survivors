Sprint 0 (foundation), Sprint 1 (movement and combat foundation), Sprint 2 (base spell system) and Sprint 3 (spell Areas), Sprint 4 (elemental enemies), Sprint 5 (scroll collection), Sprint 6 (Lectern and crafting), Sprint 7 (buffs), Sprint 8 (XP, levels and saving), Sprint 9 (Gold, equipment and the shop), Sprint 10 (potions), Sprint 11 (enemy variety, bosses and difficulty) and Sprint 12a (the world: map as data) and Sprint 12b (obstacles) and Sprint 12c (the map's regions) are done. ## Architecture (big picture)

The core idea is that spells are **assembled at runtime from data**, never hardcoded per combination. A spell is a **Base** (one or more elements: Fire/Thunder/Land/Sea), exactly one **Area** (Line/Cone/Area/Missile), and optional **Buffs**.

Layering, per `technical-design.md`:

- **Scenes** are physical objects and UI (Player, Enemy, Projectile, Lectern, HUD, crafting menu).
- **Scripts** hold logic and systems.
- **Custom `Resource`s** hold all data definitions: `SpellData`, scroll, `EnemyData`, equipment and potion definitions.
- **Autoloads** are only for truly global or persistent state (Game/Run/Save managers). Avoid adding more.

Systems that must stay separate and reusable:

- **Spell Definition vs. Spell Instance.** `SpellData` is static configuration. A spell instance is the equipped runtime object with its own mana pool. Two instances of the same definition must never share mana.
- **Spell Builder.** It computes final damage, max mana, mana cost, cast interval, area dimensions, and buff effects. It must be independent of the crafting UI and the Lectern scene.
- **Crafting System.** It validates a combination, consumes scrolls, and creates or equips the spell. Invalid combinations must not consume scrolls. The Lectern is only the interaction point.
- **Damage System.** Elemental math is centralized in one place. Spells and enemies must not compute their own. Multipliers are configurable, not hardcoded. Several Base types combine by **best match**: a hit uses whichever of the spell's elements does best against the enemy (strong beats neutral beats weak, the first wins a tie), computed only in `DamageSystem.resolve`.
- **Area behaviors.** Each Area is an interchangeable implementation behind one casting interface. Modifiers like Duplicate Area must not require editing individual Areas.
- **Progression.** Run progression (XP, levels, slot unlocks) is separate from permanent progression (Gold, equipment, saves).
- **Equipment.** Effects go through one central modifier system, not direct edits to player stats. Hats and Hoods differ only visually.
- **Player, UI, and inventory.** The Player delegates to systems and holds no spell, enemy, or crafting logic. UI reads from systems and never mutates gameplay data directly. The scroll inventory is independent of the crafting UI.

## Established Design Rules (do not change without asking)

- **Mana never regenerates naturally.** Each spell has its own pool. A depleted spell can't be cast until something explicitly restores mana (potions, debug tools, or future effects). Spells must not cast with insufficient mana.
- Crafting needs at least one Base and exactly one Area. Buffs are optional. Every buff and every increase in range or area raises mana cost. Hybrid (multi-Base) spells drain mana faster.
- Elements: Fire > Land > Thunder > Sea > Fire. Each element is strong against the one it beats and weak against the one that beats it. Enemies without an affinity take normal damage.
- XP feeds a permanent 20-level wizard track (reward table in the README). Gold buys equipment between runs. Spell slots go from 1 to 5.
- Inputs go through the Godot Input Map using logical actions. Keyboard, mouse, and controller share one gameplay implementation (WASD, E interact, 1–5 spell slots, mouse aim, LMB cast, Esc pause).

## Conventions

- Naming: PascalCase classes. snake_case variables, functions, and signals. UPPER_SNAKE_CASE constants.
- Use typed GDScript where practical. One responsibility per script. Prefer composition over inheritance. Avoid circular dependencies. Use signals between independent systems.
- Gameplay and balance values belong in Resources or `@export` properties, not scattered through scripts.
- Directories from the proposed layout (`scenes/`, `scripts/{autoload,systems,utilities}/`, `resources/`, `assets/`, `tests/`) are created only as their systems are built.
- Don't optimize prematurely. Profile first. Object pooling for projectiles is the likely first optimization.
- Record significant architectural decisions. The layout in `technical-design.md` mentions a `development-log.md`, which doesn't exist yet.

## Combat architecture (Sprint 1)

- Combat is built from small components on an entity: `HealthComponent`, `HurtboxComponent` (receives hits, optional player-wide invulnerability window), `HitboxComponent` (deals damage, per-target cooldown, optional one-shot), `KnockbackComponent`, `HitFlashComponent`. All damage goes through `DamageSystem.calculate`, the single place elemental rules will live.
- Collision layers (named in `project.godot`, bit constants in `PhysicsLayers`): world, player_hurtbox, enemy_hurtbox, enemy_hitbox, player_projectile. Enemy hurtboxes and hitboxes are passive (`monitoring = false`), so only the player's hurtbox and projectiles do any overlap work. Enemies never detect each other.
- Enemies are light `Node2D`s moved by position (no `move_and_slide()`), clamped to the arena bounds. Since Sprint 12b they also stay out of obstacles and route around them (see Obstacles). Stats come from `EnemyData` and spawning from `SpawnConfig`. The health bar and spell bar are marked TEMPORARY and expected to change in later sprints.
- Aim and fire go through `AimComponent` (mouse, right stick, or the touch aim stick) and `cast`. The Player forwards both to `SpellLoadout`. `emulate_mouse_from_touch` is off on purpose, so touching the move stick never fires.
- Hand-written `.tscn` files need `node_paths=PackedStringArray("prop")` on the `[node]` line for any exported Node-typed property set with a NodePath, or it stays null silently.
- `TouchJoystick` is ours. Godot 4.7 has a native `VirtualJoystick`, so don't reuse that name.

## Spell architecture (Sprint 2)

- `SpellData` (Resource) is static configuration: elements, damage, mana, cast interval, projectile stats, `max_hits`. `SpellInstance` (`RefCounted`) is the equipped runtime object and owns current mana and the cooldown. Never put mutable state on `SpellData`, because resources are shared and two copies of a spell must never share mana.
- `SpellLoadout` (component on the Player) holds five slots and the selected index, selects and cycles, and casts through `try_cast`. A depleted spell stays selected and does nothing (no auto-switch). Slot keys, controller shoulder buttons (`spell_prev`/`spell_next`) and the touch `SpellBar` all end in `select` or `cycle`. `try_cast` instantiates the spell's Area scene (see Sprint 3 below).
- `MissileArea` takes everything from the `SpellData` it is launched with. `HitboxComponent.max_hits` and `SpellData.max_hits` make piercing a data change.
- Elemental math lives only in `ElementTable` (configurable multipliers, Fire > Land > Thunder > Sea > Fire) and `DamageSystem.calculate(base, attacker_element, defender_element, table)`. Since Sprint 4 the multiplier is applied at impact (see Elemental enemies below).
- `SpellBar` and `SpellSlotWidget` are TEMPORARY HUD. Slot widgets read the loadout and handle touch taps themselves (`emulate_mouse_from_touch` is off) and ignore mouse clicks. F9 (`debug_restore_mana`, handled in `Main`) refills mana for testing.

## Area architecture (Sprint 3)

- Five Areas: Line, Cone, Area (ground zone), Missile, Wave. Each is its own scene under `scenes/spells/areas/`, whose root script extends `AreaEffect` and implements `launch(spell, origin, direction, target)`. `SpellLoadout.try_cast` instantiates `spell.area_data.scene` and calls it. Never branch on the Area type in the loadout, player or damage code.
- `AreaData` (Resource, `resources/spells/areas/`) holds one Area type's scene and defaults: `default_range`, `default_size`, `duration`, `tick_interval`, `widen_rate`, `needs_ground_target`. A `SpellData` points at one and carries `range_scale` and `size_scale`. `effective_range()` and `effective_size()` are the numbers every Area reads, and Sprint 6 crafting only has to write the two scales. Mana cost stays an authored number per spell. The rule "longer range costs more" belongs to the Spell Builder (Sprint 6), not the Areas.
- Ground-targeted Areas land at `SpellData.clamp_target(origin, target)`. The mouse's target is the cursor and a stick's is a far point along the aim, so a stick lands at full range. Targeting and the `TargetReticle` read `AreaData.needs_ground_target`.
- All Areas build their hit damage through `AreaEffect._configure_hitbox`, so Sprint 4 moves the elemental multiplier to hit time in one place. Line, Cone and Wave hit each enemy once per cast and Area hits once per tick, through `HitboxComponent.hit_cooldown`.
- Enemies are clamped to the arena bounds (no negative coordinates), so Area tests cast from an offset origin.

## Elemental enemies (Sprint 4)

- The elemental multiplier is applied when a hit lands. `HitboxComponent.element` is the attacker's element (set from the spell in `AreaEffect._configure_hitbox`, which carries raw damage) and `HurtboxComponent.element` is the defender's. `HurtboxComponent.receive_hit(damage, source_position, knockback, attacker_element)` gets the final damage from `DamageSystem.calculate` and an `ElementTable.Effect` (NEUTRAL, VULNERABLE, RESISTED) from `DamageSystem.effect`, then emits `hit_received(damage, source_position, knockback, effect)`. No other code does elemental math.
- Rules: an enemy has one element. It takes the strong multiplier from the element that beats it and the weak one from the element it beats. Same element, no element, or an unknown value is normal damage.
- The player's hurtbox has no element by default and enemy contact hitboxes carry none, so hits on the player are neutral. This is today's default, not a rule: equipment (robes, hats) may later give the player an element through `HurtboxComponent.element`, so never hardcode "the player is neutral".
- `EnemyData` is the composed shape an enemy runs on (element, colour, stats, rewards, behaviour). Since Sprint 11 it is built from an archetype and an element (see Enemies and bosses below), not hand-written per enemy. There is still one enemy scene. The five old enemy files live on only as test fixtures in `tests/fixtures/enemies/` (enemies with a known element and health).
- `Enemy.damaged(position, amount, effect)` is re-emitted by `EnemySpawner.enemy_damaged`, and `Main` routes it to `DamageNumbers` (TEMPORARY look), a pooled node that draws every number in one `_draw`. Vulnerable hits show larger, yellow and "!", resisted hits smaller and grey.

## Scrolls (Sprint 5)

- `ScrollData` is one class with a category (BASE, AREA, BUFF), a rarity, a placeholder colour, and optional category fields: `element` (Base), `area_data` (Area, the Sprint 3 `AreaData`), `buff_id` and `buff` (Buff, its `BuffEffect`, Sprint 7). The 15 scrolls live in `resources/scrolls/{base,area,buff}/`. Adding a scroll is a new `.tres` plus a drop-table entry, with no code change. The inventory, drop tables and crafting only ever hold `ScrollData`.
- Drops: `EnemyData.drop_table` (a `DropTable` of `DropEntry` scroll and weight pairs, plus `drop_chance`). `DropTable.roll(rng)` never picks null, scroll-less or zero-weight entries. Tables are themed and belong to the enemy elements (`resources/enemies/elements/`): an elemental enemy weights its own element's Base scroll 12 and the other Bases 1, so it is heavily preferred but never guaranteed. Enemies carry data only. `EnemySpawner.enemy_died` is re-emitted, `Main` routes it to `ScrollDropper`, which rolls the table and places a pickup. The cap is 100 pickups, and a new drop removes the oldest.
- Pickups: `ScrollPickup` is a passive `Area2D` on layer 6 (`PhysicsLayers.PICKUP`), shaped by category (round Base, square Area, diamond Buff). One `PickupCollector` on the Player is the only active detector. Collection is automatic, a pickup can only be collected once, and a dead player collects nothing.
- `ScrollInventory` is a component on the Player (per run, stacks by scroll id, no limit): `add`, `remove` (all or nothing), `count`, `has`, `by_category`, `total_count`, and a `changed` signal. Crafting (Sprint 6) reads and consumes it through those methods.
- `ScrollToast`, `InventoryPanel` (key `I`, controller button Y) and `InventoryButton` are TEMPORARY HUD. The panel does not pause the game. F10 (`debug_give_scrolls`, handled in `Main`, DEBUG) gives 3 of every scroll.

## Crafting (Sprint 6)

- The player starts with one spell (Fire Missile, a 1000-cast pool) and four locked slots. `SpellLoadout.unlocked_slots` (5 by default, 1 on the player) says how many slots can be used. `equip(index, spell_data)` puts a fresh full-mana `SpellInstance` in an unlocked slot and emits `slot_changed`. `unlock_next()` unlocks one more slot and emits `slot_unlocked` (levels call it, see Progression below). `select` and `cycle` skip locked slots, and the `SpellBar` dims them and follows a slot when its spell is replaced.
- **All crafting rules live in two static classes.** `SpellBuilder.build(base, area, option)` makes a `SpellData`: damage, interval, speed and knockback are the Area's baseline (`AreaData.base_stats`, an `AreaStats`) times the Base scroll's multiplier (`ScrollData.modifiers`, a `BaseModifiers`). Mana cost is also times the range option's cost multiplier, and the mana pool is not, so a longer range gives fewer casts. `CraftingSystem.validate/craft/preview` check a `CraftingRequest`, consume exactly one Base and one Area scroll all-or-nothing (a failed craft never touches the inventory or loadout), and equip the spell. Fire plus Missile at Normal range equals `fire_missile.tres` in every number except the pool: the starting spell has a generous 1000, while a crafted spell uses the Area baseline of 100. Range options (Short, Normal, Long, Very long) are in `resources/crafting/crafting_rules.tres`. Nothing is hand-written per Base and Area pair.
- Buffs became real in Sprint 7 (below). Size is not chosen at crafting, only range.
- `CraftingSession` (no UI) holds the screen's choices and previews. `CraftingScreen` (TEMPORARY look) is a thin layer over it and holds no rules. It works with mouse, keyboard or controller focus, and touch through `TouchBridge`, because `emulate_mouse_from_touch` is off. The bridged click goes straight into the viewport with `push_input` (not `Input.parse_input_event`, which would scale the position twice and press the global `cast` action).
- **Pause.** Opening the screen pauses the scene tree (`CraftingScreen` has `process_mode = Always`) through `GamePause.set_paused`, which also freezes `GameTime`, a clock that stands still while paused. `HitboxComponent.try_hit` reads `GameTime.now_msec()`, so hit cooldowns survive a pause. `Main` resets `GameTime` on load. After the screen closes, `Player.require_cast_release()` keeps the player from casting until the cast input is released once (it reads the real input, because `aim.is_firing` is stale on the first frame after a pause).
- **Lecterns** are passive `Area2D`s on layer 7 (`PhysicsLayers.INTERACTABLE`) in the group `lecterns`. They hold no crafting logic and only emit `interacted`. The Player's `InteractionComponent` tracks the nearest one in range, shows its prompt, and `interact` (E, controller A) or the touch `InteractButton` uses it. A dead player cannot interact. Three Lecterns are placed by the map data (see World and maps below), far apart. They are reusable, unlimited and free.
- F8 (`debug_give_potions`, 3 of every potion, saved), F10 (`debug_give_scrolls`), F12 (`debug_give_xp`, 100 XP, saved like real XP) and F9 (`debug_restore_mana`) are DEBUG keys handled in `Main`, to delete when their real systems exist.

## Buffs (Sprint 7)

- A buff is data: `BuffEffect` (`resources/crafting/buff_effect.gd`) on the Buff scroll holds `damage_multiplier`, `max_mana_multiplier`, `cost_multiplier`, `vulnerability_bonus`, `ignores_resistance`, `extra_copies`, `extra_bases` and `max_stacks` (0 means none). The six effects are in `resources/crafting/buff_effects/`. A repeated scroll in a request is a stack. Multipliers multiply, bonuses add, and no buff is hardcoded by name anywhere.
- `SpellBuilder.build(bases, area, option, buffs)` averages the Bases' multipliers (a hybrid), then applies each stack. Every stack raises the cost at least as fast as it raises damage or copies, so damage per mana never improves. Increased Max Mana is the deliberate exception. `SpellBuilder.can_cast(spell)` is false when the cost is above the pool, and `CraftingSystem` then refuses with `REASON_TOO_COSTLY`.
- `CraftingRequest.bases` is a list. The count must equal 1 plus the Additional Base stacks, with no repeated element, and stacks respect `max_stacks`. `CraftingSystem` checks the whole recipe and the owned quantities first and only then consumes every scroll, so a failed craft still changes nothing.
- A `SpellData` carries `copies`, `vulnerability_scale`, `ignores_resistance` and `buff_stacks`. Its `attack_profile()` (cached, so call it once the spell is built) is what a hit carries. `HurtboxComponent.receive_hit` resolves it through `DamageSystem.resolve`.
- `CastPattern.shots(spell, origin, direction, target)` returns one `CastShot` per copy (a ring around the player, or a ring of zones around the target), and `SpellLoadout.try_cast` launches one effect per shot for a single mana payment. Areas know nothing about copies.
- `CraftingSession` holds `bases`, `buffs` (stacks), `max_bases()`, `add_buff`, `remove_buff`, `can_add_buff` and `drawbacks()`. `CraftingScreen` shows a buff row with steppers, the Base heading "Base (n of N)" and the drawbacks. The look is TEMPORARY.

## Progression (Sprint 8)

- XP and level are **permanent**. Everyone starts at level 1, which gives nothing. `LevelTrack` (`resources/progression/level_track.tres`) holds the XP curve, the 19 `LevelReward`s for levels 2 to 20 (SPEED, HEALTH, MANA, SLOT, RANDOM_BUFF, CHOSEN_BUFF, SECOND_SPELL) and the `buff_pool`. All of it is data, and no code branches on a level number or a buff id. The level is always computed from `PlayerProfile.total_xp`.
- **Saving.** `PlayerProfile` (total XP, Gold, owned and equipped items) is written by the static `SaveGame` as versioned JSON in `user://save.json` (outside the repo, in Godot's per-project user-data folder), through a temporary file and a rename. A missing file is a fresh profile, and a file that cannot be used is renamed to `.bak` and never deleted. `ProfileSaver` saves on request, every 10 s while dirty, and on close, focus loss and app pause. To reset progress, delete the one file.
- **Tests never touch the real save.** `SaveGame` calls take a path, and tests use a folder inside `res://.godot/save_tests/` that they create and remove. `Main.save_path` defaults to the real file, and an empty string means "do not load or save", so every test that instantiates `main.tscn` sets it (and the pick screen's `pause_tree` to false) before `add_child`.
- **When rewards apply.** Stat and slot rewards apply at once, through `RunProgress` (a Player component that turns collected XP into level-ups). Starting-spell rewards apply at the next run start. `Player.apply_stat_bonuses(StatBonuses, heal_difference)` is the one stat path: it computes speed, maximum health and the spells' mana bonus from the player's base values, so applying twice is harmless (Sprint 9 equipment will feed the same `StatBonuses`). The mana bonus lives on each `SpellInstance` (`max_mana()`), never on a shared `SpellData`, so read a pool through `max_mana()`.
- **Run start.** `Main._begin_progression` loads the profile, rolls every earned random buff (`StartingBuffs.roll_random`) and asks for each earned chosen buff on `BuffPickScreen` (the world is paused meanwhile), into an in-memory `RunBuffs`. **Starting-spell buffs are never saved: they are rolled or picked again at every run start** (player feedback). Then `RunStart.prepare` sets stats, slots, equipment bonuses and the two starting spells (`SpellBuilder.with_buffs` returns a copy with the buffs folded in, so the shared spell resources are never changed). The pool leaves out Additional Base, because a starting spell has no second Base.
- **XP gems and Gold coins.** `EnemyData.xp_reward` and `gold_reward`, one `ValueGem` pickup (kind XP or GOLD, collected by the same `PickupCollector`, which emits `xp_collected` or `gold_collected`) and one `ValueDropper` per kind (capped at 150, merging a new drop into the oldest at the cap so nothing is lost). The XP bar, Gold counter, level toast and the death screen's summary line are TEMPORARY looks.

## Gold and equipment (Sprint 9)

- **Gold** is banked in `PlayerProfile.gold` the moment a coin is collected (`RunProgress.add_gold`) and saved like XP. Dying never costs it.
- **One modifier path.** Level rewards and equipped items both add into one `StatBonuses` (`StatBonuses.combined`), pushed by `Player.apply_stat_bonuses` to speed, health and `SpellLoadout.set_bonuses`. `RunStart.prepare` and `RunProgress._apply` both use level plus `ShopSystem.bonuses`, so a level-up never drops equipment and nothing applies twice. The stats are SPEED, HEALTH, SPELL_MANA, the four element damages, SPELL_DAMAGE, CAST_SPEED and the two chance effects.
- **Casting.** Static `CastMath` holds the formulas (damage scale = spell damage times the mean of the spell's element bonuses, floored at 0.1; interval = base / (1 + casting speed), floored at 0.05 s; pool). `SpellLoadout.try_cast` sets `AreaEffect.damage_scale` on each effect before `launch` (the one multiplication is in `AreaEffect._configure_hitbox`), so no Area and no shared `SpellData` is edited. `SpellInstance` carries `cast_speed_bonus` and `max_mana_bonus` (per instance). Wand "cast twice" launches every shot a second time for the same mana and cooldown, and Staff "double damage" doubles every shot, each rolled once per cast from `SpellLoadout.rng`. `SpellBuilder.describe(spell, bonuses)` and the crafting preview use the same math.
- **Equipment is data:** `EquipmentData` (id, name, description, category, price, `EquipmentModifier` list, colour) in an `EquipmentCatalog` (`resources/equipment/`). Three slots: Robe, Weapon (Wand or Staff) and Headwear (Hat or Hood, identical behaviour). Adding an item is a new `.tres` plus a catalog entry. No code branches on an item id.
- **Shop.** The static `ShopSystem` holds all the rules (`buy` is all-or-nothing and equips into the item's slot, `equip` and `unequip` are free, `bonuses` skips unknown, unowned or misplaced ids). `ShopScreen` (TEMPORARY look) is a thin layer over it, opened by the death screen's Shop button, and every successful action is saved at once. Pressing an item buys, equips or takes it off depending on what the player has.
- **Death-screen taps.** Touches never reach buttons (mouse emulation is off), so `DeathScreen.handle_event` handles a tap on the Shop button itself (it requests the shop and never restarts), ignores every restart while the shop is open (`set_shop_open`), and still restarts on a tap anywhere else. This was checked on a real window, not only headless.
- Tests that instantiate `main.tscn` set `save_path` and never touch the real save, as in Sprint 8.

## Potions (Sprint 10)

- **Potions are data:** `PotionData` (id, name, description, price, `kind` HEAL / RESTORE_MANA / TIMED_BONUS / INVULNERABILITY, magnitude, duration, cooldown, `EquipmentModifier`s, colour) in a `PotionCatalog` (`resources/potions/`, bar order). No code branches on a potion id. Adding a potion of an existing kind is a new `.tres` plus a catalog entry.
- **Stock and saving.** `PlayerProfile.potions` (id to count, 1 to 99, zero counts removed). Buying is `ShopSystem.buy_potion` (one at a time, all or nothing). The shop screen has a Potions row. Drinking saves at once (`Main` saves on `PotionBelt.used`), so quitting cannot refund a potion. Unused potions survive death.
- **Drinking.** Static `PotionSystem` holds the rules: `refusal` returns why a potion can't be drunk (checked in a fixed order: unknown, dead, none left, type cooldown, shared cooldown, effect running, full health, full mana) and `apply` does the effect. A refused potion is never consumed and starts no cooldown. `PotionBelt` (Player component) drinks through it, keeps each type's cooldown and the 1 s shared cooldown, and holds the controller's selection. Cooldowns and effect timers tick in `_process`, so they freeze while the tree is paused.
- **Timed effects.** `ActiveEffects` (Player component) keeps the running TIMED_BONUS and INVULNERABILITY effects and owns the player's temporary bonus layer. Invulnerability also grants the hurtbox a separate window (`HurtboxComponent.grant_invulnerability`) that the per-hit window can neither shorten nor clear.
- **Stat layers.** The Player holds `level_bonuses`, `equipment_bonuses` and `temporary_bonuses`. Each owner sets its own layer (`RunStart` and `RunProgress` the level one, `RunStart` the equipment one, `ActiveEffects` the temporary one) and calls `Player.refresh_bonuses()`, which adds the three, pushes the total through `apply_stat_bonuses` and emits `bonuses_changed`. Nothing else pushes bonuses.
- **Luck** is a `StatBonuses` stat. `DropTable.roll(rng, luck)` multiplies a scroll's weight by `rarity_factor` (Common 1, Uncommon 1 + luck, Rare 1 + 2 x luck, never below 0; falls back to luck 0 if every weight would be zero), and `Main` keeps `ScrollDropper.luck` in step with `bonuses_changed`. At luck 0 the roll is exactly the old one.
- **The potion bar** (`PotionBar`, `PotionSlotWidget`, TEMPORARY look) is a column of six slots at the top right. Slots handle their own touches and left clicks (mouse emulation is off), and `Main` blocks casting until the cast input is released so a click never also fires a spell. Keys Z X C V B N (`potion_1` to `potion_6`), controller X, left stick click and B (`potion_next`, `potion_prev`, `potion_use`; the D-pad is movement, and a test fails if any joypad button belongs to two gameplay actions). A refusal shows its reason in the level toast's message line. The bar hides on death.
- Checked on a real window (3x scale) with a throwaway profile: a tap or a mouse click on a slot drinks exactly once and fires no spell.

## Enemies and bosses (Sprint 11)

- **An enemy is an archetype plus an element, picked independently by weight (user decision, "mix and match").** `EnemyArchetype` (`resources/enemies/archetypes/`: Standard, Swarmer, Chaser, Tank, Ranged, Exploder, Support) holds every stat, the body size, the behaviour (`EnemyBehaviorData`), the unlock time, the cluster size and the drop-chance scale. `EnemyElement` (`resources/enemies/elements/`: none, Fire, Thunder, Land, Sea) holds only the affinity, the colour and the themed drop table. **An element never changes a stat**: a Thunder Tank is as tough as a Land Tank. `EnemyComposer` builds one cached, shared `EnemyData` per pair (names like "Thunder Tank").
- **Shared data is never edited.** Per-spawn values live on the `Enemy` instance: `apply_scales` (difficulty) and `make_elite(EliteModifier)` (x3 health, x1.5 damage, x1.15 speed, x1.4 size, x3 rewards, x2 drop chance, an outline), both worked out from base values so they never compound. `xp_reward()`, `gold_reward()` and `drop_chance_scale()` are what the droppers read, and `gives_rewards` is false for an Exploder that blasted itself.
- **Behaviour is a brain, chosen by the archetype's kind.** `EnemyBrain.for_data` gives `BrainChase` (the old movement), `BrainKeepDistance` (Ranged: holds about 90 px and fires `EnemyProjectile`s), `BrainExplode` (stops, fuses with a `BlastZone`, dies in its blast with no rewards, and the blast is cancelled if the player kills it first) or `BrainSupport` (heals other enemies in range every 0.25 s). Nothing branches on an enemy name.
- **Telegraph rule.** `EnemyProjectile` (cap 60) and `BlastZone` (cap 40) are shared by enemies and the boss. A blast zone is harmless until its fuse ends, never moves, checks the player once, and counts `_process` deltas so it freezes while paused. Every boss attack has a warning.
- **Difficulty.** `DifficultyCurve` (`resources/enemies/difficulty_curve.tres`) maps run seconds to health, damage and speed scales, the spawn interval, the maximum alive, the elite chance and the boss interval. `RunClock` (static) is the pausable run clock: it reads `GameTime` from a start marker that `Main` resets (`GameTime.reset()` alone does not zero it). The spawner unlocks archetypes by time, spawns Swarmer clusters, and `spawn_rate_scale` halves spawning while a boss lives.
- **Bosses are data.** `BossData` (phases, rewards) with `BossPhase` (a health threshold, an attack list, an interval, a speed multiplier) and `BossAttack` (SLAM, CHARGE, BURST, SUMMON with their numbers). `Boss` is one state machine (IDLE, TELEGRAPH, ACT, RECOVER) that runs any `BossData`: it ignores knockback, stays in the arena, takes +50% damage after a charge, and a hit that skips a phase lands in the last one it reached. `RunDirector` spawns a boss at 300 s and 300 s after the previous one spawned (never two at once), cycles the roster, scales each boss by its number and pays XP, Gold (both times the number) and Rare scrolls where it died. The run continues after a boss (user decision).
- **Stress.** The stress scene starts the clock at `--minutes` (default 5), has `--boss`, and keeps its enemy count with `spawn_at(position, cluster_limit)`.

## World and maps (Sprint 12a)

- **A map is data.** `MapData` (`resources/maps/`) holds the size, colours, player start, `spawn_config` (null keeps the scene's), `placements` (`MapPlacement`: a scene and a position) and the decoration scatter fields. `problems()` lists faults. The first map is `first_map.tres`: 1920×1080, start (960, 540), three Lecterns at least 700 px apart. Nothing branches on a map id.
- **`GameMap`** (`scenes/world/game_map.tscn`) builds the `Border` and `Floor` rects, the four boundary walls (static bodies on the world layer, closed at the corners), the `Decor` layer, the `Placements` and a hidden `Debug` overlay from its `map_data`. It replaced `test_arena.tscn` in `main.tscn`. `test_arena.tscn` stays only for the stress scene, so the stress numbers stay comparable.
- **`Main._apply_map` is the only place the arena size is read:** it sets `EnemySpawner.arena_bounds`, the spawner's config (when the map has one), the player's start and the camera limits. The `Rect2(0, 0, 960, 540)` defaults in `EnemySpawner`, `Enemy` and `Boss`, and the tests' own bounds, are only defaults.
- **Decorations** are `MapDecor.scatter(map)`: seeded, deterministic, clear of the player start and every placement, and it always ends. `MapDecorLayer` draws them in one `_draw` (TEMPORARY look).
- **F7** (`debug_toggle_map`, handled in `Main`, DEBUG) toggles `MapDebugOverlay` (bounds, start, a labelled marker per placement). Sprints 12b and 12c add obstacles and points of interest as more placements and data.
- Obstacles (Sprint 12b, below) are placements-in-data too: `MapData.obstacles`.

## Obstacles (Sprint 12b)

- **What obstacles block (user decision):** movement (the player's and every enemy's) and enemy projectiles. **Every player spell passes over them**, so no Area and no damage code knows about obstacles. Blast zones and boss slams are ground effects and ignore them. Missile blocking was considered and rejected.
- **Data.** `MapObstacle` (`resources/maps/`): RECT (centre, full size) or CIRCLE (centre, radius), with exact `contains`, `overlaps_rect` and `blocks_segment`. `MapData.obstacles`, `nav_cell_size` (16) and `body_clearance` (8) hold them. `MapData.problems()` checks shapes (valid, inside the map, 24 px from the start and every placement). **Reachability is `ObstacleGrid.reachability_problems(map)`, not `problems()`** (the grid is built from `MapData`, so the other way round would be a circular dependency), and `GameMap` reports both with `push_error`.
- **`ObstacleGrid`** is a coarse byte grid: a cell is blocked when an obstacle grown by the body clearance touches it, and cells outside the map count as blocked. **`FlowField`** sweeps outward from the player over the free cells (Dial's algorithm, the 16 moves of the 5x5 chamfer, never cutting a blocked cell) and each cell's direction is the pull of its lower neighbours. When two routes tie, or the blended direction would point into a blocked cell, the single best neighbour is used (found by the U-shaped dead-end test: the symmetric tie jammed enemies on the axis). Directions are cached per cell until the next field is swapped in.
- **The sweep is incremental.** A whole sweep of the first map takes about 33 ms, so `FlowField.begin_rebuild` and `advance(cell_budget)` do it in pieces (about 400 cells, 1.8 ms, a frame; 19 frames, so the field lags the player by about 0.3 s) and the finished field replaces the old one all at once. `ObstacleMap` starts a rebuild when the player enters a new cell and at least 0.2 s have passed since the last one began. Physics frames stop while paused, so nothing advances then.
- **`ObstacleMap`** (a node under `GameMap`, in the group `obstacle_map`) owns the grid and the field and answers `direction_from`, `is_blocked`, `blocks_segment` (exact against the shapes, with a quick all-cells-free shortcut for short segments), `push_out` and `resolve_move` (the move, else the free axis, else stay). **With no `ObstacleMap` in the tree every caller behaves as before**, which is why older tests did not change. `GameMap` also builds a `StaticBody2D` per obstacle on the world layer (the player collides through physics with the same shapes) and an `ObstacleLayer` that draws them in one `_draw` (TEMPORARY look).
- **Who uses it.** `Enemy.direction_to_target` follows the field (keeping the horde-spreading offset as a sideways nudge) and `_physics_process` moves through `resolve_move`, so Chase, Ranged's approach, the Exploder and Support all route with no brain edited. The one brain that checks a line is Ranged (`Enemy.has_clear_line_to`): behind a wall it keeps approaching through the field and does not fire, instead of holding its distance (found in the review: it parked behind walls). A knocked-back or spawned-inside enemy is pushed out on its next step. The `Boss` walks the same field, and a Charge that hits an obstacle ends at once (the recovery and the vulnerability window still follow). `EnemyProjectile` frees itself when its step crosses an obstacle. `EnemySpawner` rejects blocked ring points and pushes every spawned enemy, cluster member, summon and boss out of obstacles.
- **Layout limits** (tested in `tests/test_first_map_layout.gd`): 25 to 35 obstacles, the outer 48 px band free, a 150 px open area around the start, 3% to 25% of cells blocked, no gap narrower than two cells (16 px cells and an 8 px clearance can turn a 48 px corridor into one free cell, so corridor walls are 80 px apart centre to centre and off the grid), at least one open 10 by 10 cell square 400 px from the start. Every Lectern is judged by eye to have two routes.
- **F7** also draws the obstacle outlines, the blocked cells and the field's arrows near the player. `--obstacles` on the stress scene uses the first map.

- **Near the player, bodies use exact walls and the real player position (two bug fixes during Sprint 12c).** The grid keeps bodies 8 to 24 px off walls (the clearance plus rounding to 16 px cells) and the flow field is built from where the player was a moment ago (it rebuilds over about 0.3 s), but the player stands 6 px from walls and moves every frame. Two exploits came from that: hiding in the corner of two walls (or pressed against a thin wall), and hugging a corridor wall while running back and forth (enemies followed the field down the middle, chasing where the player had been). So within `ObstacleMap.NEAR_RANGE` (160 px) of the player: `Enemy.direction_to_target` and the boss steer straight at the player when `ObstacleMap.line_clear` says a body can walk the line (the grid answers most lines, a line through a blocked cell is checked against the exact shapes grown by `MapData.body_radius`, 5 px, a little under half a body so a body flush against a wall is not blocked by a rounding error), and `resolve_move` lets a body enter a blocked cell if it does not overlap an exact wall (`ObstacleMap.overlaps_wall`). Beyond it, and with no clear line, the field routes as before, and a body inside a blocked cell uses the direction of the nearest free cell (`direction_from`), so it never pushes straight at a wall. **The invariant to keep: the player can only stand where an enemy can reach, and the field is only for routing.**

## Map regions (Sprint 12c)

- **`MapMaze`** (`resources/maps/`) generates a maze from data: `area`, `cell_size` (80, wall centre to wall centre, so a 16 px wall leaves a 64 px gap), `wall_thickness`, `maze_seed`, `entrances` (`MazeEntrance`: a boundary cell and an `Edge`), `extra_openings` (walls removed after generating, for loops) and `clear_center` (opens the middle 2 by 2 cells). It is an iterative recursive backtracker with a local seeded `RandomNumberGenerator`, so the same data always gives the same walls, in the same order. `shapes()` returns RECT obstacles. `MazeEntrance.Edge` is not called `Side` because that is a Godot global enum. The seed field is `maze_seed`, because `seed` shadows a built-in function.
- **`MapData.solid_shapes()` is the one list of solid things**: the authored `obstacles` plus every maze's walls, built fresh on each call. `ObstacleGrid.build`, `GameMap` (the drawn layer and the physics bodies), `ObstacleMap` (it keeps the list from `setup`), `MapDebugOverlay`, `MapDecor` and `MapData.problems()` all read it, and nothing else knows mazes exist. `problems()` reports a bad maze or zone and skips the shape checks of a maze that already has problems, so one fault is one line.
- **`MapZone`** is a region with a floor tint and its own decorations (no gameplay effect yet). `GameMap` draws a `Zones` node (one `ColorRect` per zone, ignoring touches) above `Floor` and below `Decor`. `MapDecor.scatter` and `scatter_zone` keep every decoration off the player start, the placements and every solid shape (2 px margin).
- **The first map** is still 1920x1080, laid out in a 3x3 grid of regions of about 640x360: **two 9x6 cell mazes in opposite corners** (north-west `Rect2(60, 60, 720, 480)`, south-east `Rect2(1140, 540, 720, 480)`; the second has the same data with `rotated = true`, so it is an exact half-turn image of the first), each with entrances on three sides, 3 extra openings and a cleared centre room (`MapMaze.center_room_center()` gives its middle, which follows the rotation), a walled island in the north-east (about 360x280, a gate south and a gate west, each 80 px, an empty interior), a 560 px corridor along the bottom, the open plaza between the mazes and four pillar groves. The three Lecterns are outside the mazes and the island. Each maze's centre room and the island's interior are left empty for Sprint 12d.
- **Performance work found by profiling** (Sprint 12c, two mazes): `MapMaze.shapes()` merges walls that continue along one line into one rectangle (the same area, about 40% fewer shapes: physics bodies, grid build and shape tests all scale with the count), and `MapDecorLayer` and `ObstacleLayer` draw in 512 px `DrawChunk` nodes so the engine skips the ones off screen (one big node was drawn whole every frame, about 3 ms). `item_count()` is still the total.
- **Layout tests** (`test_first_map_layout.gd`, `test_first_map_regions.gd`): 50 to 160 solid shapes, the outer 48 px band and a 150 px area around the start free, 3% to 30% of cells blocked, no gap narrower than two cells, **every free cell reachable from the start**, an open 10 by 10 cell square 400 px from the start. Coordinates sit off the 16 px grid on purpose (a maze at x = 100 and y = 60), because a bad alignment turns a 48 px corridor into one free cell.

## Web build and GitHub Pages

- `.github/workflows/deploy-web.yml` builds the `Web` export preset with Godot 4.7.2 and publishes it with GitHub Pages. It runs when `main` changes and from the Actions tab ("Run workflow"). It downloads the Godot editor and the export templates for the version pinned in the workflow's `GODOT_RELEASE`, checks them against Godot's published SHA-512 sums, and caches the editor and the two web templates it needs. **When the project moves to a new Godot version, change `GODOT_VERSION` and `GODOT_RELEASE` there.**
- One-time setup (a repository setting, not code): Settings > Pages > Build and deployment > Source: **GitHub Actions**. The game is then at `https://jtexpo.github.io/GODOT-SpellCraft-Survivors/` (the last part changes if the repository is renamed).
- The preset is single-threaded (`variant/thread_support=false`, no extensions), because GitHub Pages cannot send the cross-origin isolation headers a threaded web build needs. PWA mode is off on purpose, so a phone never serves a stale cached build while testing. `exclude_filter` keeps GUT, the tests, the docs, `.github`, `venv` and `export` out of the package.
- Web builds need the `gl_compatibility` renderer, which the project already uses. The exported package is small (about 225 KB) and the engine file (`index.wasm`) is about 39 MB uncompressed.
- The `export/` folder is gitignored. To check an export on a machine **without installing templates into Godot's own folder**, make a throwaway copy of the project (for example with `rsync`, leaving out `.git`, `venv`, `.godot` and `export`), unpack only `templates/web_nothreads_release.zip` and `web_nothreads_debug.zip` from the `.tpz` into a scratch folder, and in the copy's `export_presets.cfg` set `custom_template/release` and `custom_template/debug` to those zip paths. Then run `godot --headless --path <copy> --import` and `godot --headless --path <copy> --export-release "Web" <copy>/export/web/index.html`, and serve that folder with `python -m http.server` on 127.0.0.1. Delete the copy afterwards.
- The F9, F10 and F12 debug keys are keyboard-only, so a phone has no way to give itself scrolls or XP. Touch testing means playing for drops.

## Touch and input gotchas

- A `ColorRect` (like any `Control`) stops mouse and touch input by default (`mouse_filter` STOP), and a visible one swallows the event before `_unhandled_input` sees it. World decorations (the arena floor, the player and enemy bodies, Lectern parts, projectile bodies) must set `mouse_filter = 2` (IGNORE). `tests/test_world_input.gd` fails if a world scene gets a Control that stops touches. A phone's "tap to restart" was broken for exactly this reason: the arena floor covered the whole screen.
- Anything that must react to a tap anywhere (the death screen) listens in `_input`, which runs before any control, not in `_unhandled_input`. Widgets that handle their own touches (`SpellSlotWidget`, the Inventory and Interact buttons, `TouchJoystick`) already do.
- In headless GUT runs pointer events (mouse and touch) reach `_input` but never `_unhandled_input`, while keys reach both. So a headless test cannot show a swallowed tap. Check touch behaviour with a throwaway real-window script that sends `Input.parse_input_event` touches in window coordinates (multiply the game position by `root.get_final_transform()`), as was done for the touch bridge and for this bug.
