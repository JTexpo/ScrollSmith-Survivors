# The three maps' content

The forest and the Tempest Dragon, the ruins' art refresh, the maze and the Dire Minotaur. Moved word for word from `CLAUDE.md`, one `##` section per sprint. Sections name each other (for example "see Obstacles"); find them in this folder (the index is in `CLAUDE.md`, "Where the detail lives").

## The forest's art and the Tempest Dragon (Sprint 16b)

- **A map names its boss roster.** `MapData.bosses` (an array, empty by default) overrides the shared spawn config's roster; the spawn config stays one shared resource. `Main._apply_map` sets `EnemySpawner.boss_roster`, `EnemySpawner.bosses()` returns it (else `config.bosses`, else empty), and `RunDirector`, the stress scene, the soak driver and `MapEntry.boss_name()` (the map card's boss line) all read that. The forest lists the Tempest Dragon; the ruins send the Ember Titan and, since 16d, the maze sends the Dire Minotaur.
- **The new attack, `BossAttack.Kind.LIGHTNING`** (appended to the enum so saved numbers stay valid; new field `spread`, default 80). `LightningPlan.points(center, count, spread, min_gap, bounds, rng)` (pure, seeded) gives the strike points: the first on the player's spot when the warning starts (clamped into the arena), the rest the best of 20 candidates within `spread`, preferring the one farthest from the strikes already placed. Each strike is an ordinary `BlastZone` (the telegraph rule, the cap of 40 and the sounds already exist); `Boss` keeps `_zones` and `lightning_zones()`, plays `telegraph` then a short `attack` pose, and `_on_died` cancels every undetonated strike. `BossAttack.escape_margin(player_speed)` is `speed * telegraph - (spread + radius)`; **a test pins it above zero at the player's base speed (90) for every lightning attack in the Dragon's data, so a player running straight away can always leave every strike**.
- **The Tempest Dragon** (`resources/bosses/tempest_dragon.tres`): Thunder, the Titan's weight (1500 health, 20 contact, body scale 3, 300 XP, 150 Gold, 2 scrolls from the Rare table) with its own pattern: speed 36 (the Titan 30) and three phases, 100% (interval 2.4: lightning of 3 strikes, charge, summon of Swarmers), 60% (1.6, speed x1.2: lightning of 5, burst of 8, charge, summon) and 25% (1.1, speed x1.4: lightning of 7, burst of 12, a faster charge). Starting values for the user to tune by play (the lightning warnings are 1.3, 1.4 and 1.4 s, so a player always has at least a quarter of a second to react: a test pins `escape_margin >= speed x 0.25`). It walks like the Titan (the flow field, trees stop it); a flying boss would need new movement rules.
- **Forest enemy sprites.** `enemy_forest_swarmer` (rats, 10 by 10), `enemy_forest_tank` (an ent, 24 by 24), `enemy_forest_exploder` (creepers, 14 by 14, with `fuse`) and `enemy_forest_support` (a shaman, 14 by 14, with `pulse`), each with `move` and `death` and the five element colourways, found by the 16a `enemy_<set>_<type>` lookup. The goblin, wolves and kobolds (standard, chaser, ranged) still show through the fallback, and so do the ruins and the maze. The art was composed with a scratchpad generator (simple shapes plus an automatic 1 px outline, so the margin rule holds); the committed `.sprite` grids are the source. The ent first read as a mushroom (a smooth crown) and the rat as a dark blob, so both were redrawn after looking at contact sheets.
- **The Dragon's art** (`boss_tempest_dragon`, 40 by 32, fixed colours from the existing palette, no new letters): a blue storm dragon with a yellow belly and lightning-bolt horns; `idle`, `telegraph` (rearing, mouth open, bolts), `attack` (a white flash) and `vulnerable` (drooped wings, stars).
- **Checked.** A real window (a forest crowd, the Dragon, the lightning warning and strike, the vulnerable pose) showed the forest not bare, so no world art was added. A kiting bot (the starting Fire Missile, the boss alone, three seeds) died every time but lasted 43 to 57 s against the Dragon and 27 to 30 s against the Titan, so the Dragon is the gentler fight, as the forest being the easiest map suggests; nothing was changed. Stress with `--boss` on the forest (the Dragon; the stress scene now sends the map's own boss, which the first measurement missed, so it had measured the Titan) ran at the ruins' rate (29, 40 and 36 against 37, 33 and 30 fps, throttled laptop).
- **Test pitfall:** the 16a `test_enemy_art_sets` "no such sprite" test used the forest set, which now has sprites; it uses an unknown set instead.

## The ruins' art refresh (Sprint 16c)

- **Three new ruins sprites,** found by the 16a `enemy_<set>_<type>` lookup with no code change: `enemy_ruins_standard` (a bandit: a masked rogue in a bandana with a cloak, belt and dagger, 14 by 14), `enemy_ruins_chaser` (a mimic: a treasure chest with a toothy lid and a red tongue that chomps, 12 by 12) and `enemy_ruins_ranged` (an elemental: a floating spirit with a bright core that flares as it throws, 14 by 14, with `attack`). Same sizes, animations, fps and loop flags as the sprites beside them, five element colourways. The ruins column is now bandit, slime, mimic, animated armour, elemental, mephit (the imp) and cultist.
- **Nothing was renamed (the user's option A), and in 16d the planned rename was dropped.** The plain `enemy_<type>` sprites stay as the default and the fallback for ever: the empty art set, the stress scene and many tests rely on those names and they keep "a missing sprite never leaves a coloured square" true. The goblin, wolf and kobold are still the forest's standard, chaser and ranged, and the slime, armour, imp and cultist the ruins' swarmer, tank, exploder and support; the maze has all seven of its own since 16d.
- **Art method as in 16b:** composed with a scratchpad generator (shapes plus an automatic 1 px outline), the committed `.sprite` grids are the source. Looked at contact sheets: the first bandit was a hooded figure too close to the cultist, so it got a bandana band, a mask and a dagger and was compared side by side with the cultist and goblin.
- **Found by the user: the mimic did not animate.** Its two move frames were identical (a bug in my generator), and the sheets looked fine because I only looked at one frame. `test_ruins_art` now requires at least 8 differing pixels between the two frames of every move, death and attack animation of the new sprites; the mimic now snaps its jaw and moves its legs. **When looking at a sprite sheet, compare the frames of each animation, not just the first.**
- **Only the look changed:** `test_art_set_is_only_a_look` pins that the composed enemy data, speed, hit shapes and scale are the same for every art set and that the ranged brain plays `attack` on the new elemental. Two older tests that assumed the ruins use the plain sprites for all seven types were updated (`test_forest_art`, `test_enemy_art_sets`).
- **Checked in a real window:** a 40-enemy crowd on the ruins and on the forest.

## The maze's art and the Dire Minotaur (Sprint 16d)

- **Chained charges.** `BossAttack.repeats` (extra dashes after the first, default 0) and `chain_warning` (seconds of warning before each chained dash, default 0.6) and `dash_count()`. `Boss` keeps `_dashes_left`: when a dash runs its time with dashes left it re-aims at where the player is now (`_chain_next`, `_aim_dash`), goes back to TELEGRAPH for `chain_warning` showing the dash-line marks, then dashes again; **a dash that hits an obstacle ends the whole chain at once (the boss crashes and is open)**; the vulnerability window follows the last dash only; `attack_started` fires once per attack. The Titan's and Dragon's charges have `repeats` 0 and behave exactly as before (a test pins it).
- **The Dire Minotaur** (`resources/bosses/dire_minotaur.tres`, listed in `maze_map.tres`'s `bosses`): Land, the Titan's weight (1500 health, 20 contact, body scale 3, 300 XP, 150 Gold, 2 scrolls from the Rare table), speed 32, three phases: 100% (interval 2.6: slam, a charge with `repeats` 1, a Swarmer summon), 60% (1.8, speed x1.2: slam, a charge with `repeats` 2, a burst of 10, summon), 25% (1.2, x1.4: a faster charge with `repeats` 2, slam, a burst of 12). Starting values for the user to tune. **A test pins the always-avoidable rule for every chained charge:** `(chain_warning - 0.25 s reaction) x the player's base speed 90 >= the boss's half hit width (12 px x body scale 3 / 2 = 18) + the player's 6 px`.
- **The maze's seven enemy sprites** (`enemy_maze_<type>`): a zombie (standard, a big white eye against a sunken one, a toothy mouth, ribs showing through torn clothes, arms raised), snakes (swarmer), a ghost (chaser), a troll (tank: a big head set low between narrower shoulders with moss patches, a rounded belly, a bone necklace and thick darker arms), a skeleton archer (ranged, with `attack`), a warforge (exploder, with `fuse`: a robot with a glowing furnace chest) and a beholder (support, with `pulse`). Same sizes, animations, fps and loop flags as the plain sprites, five colourways. The Minotaur's art (`boss_dire_minotaur`, 40 by 32, fixed palette colours) was redrawn twice on the user's feedback (the first had an axe that grew out of the shoulder, then a plain-brown body with empty corners beside the neck): the final is a symmetrical escaped prisoner with thick curved cream horns, glowing red eyes under a brow, a gold nose ring, spiked steel shoulder pads, an iron belt with a gold skull buckle and broken shackles with a hanging chain on each wrist.
- **No renaming** (see the 16c section): the maze simply adds its own set and the plain sprites stay.
- **The animation audit.** `tests/test_animation_frames.gd` checks every two-frame animation of every enemy and boss sprite differs by at least 4 pixels (`test_maze_art` and `test_ruins_art` require 8 for the new sprites): identical frames are always a bug (the 16c mimic). It found no static animation in any older sprite.
- **Checked.** `test_dire_minotaur_fight`: the maze sends the Minotaur; a fight on open ground uses every attack, runs a three-dash stampede, respects the blast zone cap and dies once; its rewards drop once; **the Minotaur crosses the maze to the player**: from the farthest dead end it took 169 simulated seconds and never stood still for more than 0.2 s (the test's bounds are 5 minutes and 20 s). A real window (a maze crowd, a stampede warning with its dash-line marks in a corridor, the three bosses side by side) read well and the maze did not look bare, so no world art was added. Balance (a kiting bot with the starting spell, three seeds, died every time): it lasted 30 to 55 s against the Minotaur, 43 to 57 s against the Dragon and 27 to 30 s against the Titan; **every one of the Minotaur's chains in the maze was cut short by a wall** (9 of 9), so in corridors a stampede is one dash and a crash that opens the boss, as designed (the extra dashes only happen where there is room). Stress with `--boss --map=maze` ran at or above the ruins' rate in every pair of runs (throttled laptop).
- **Facing convention (found by the user: "the ghost runs backwards"):** every sprite is drawn facing RIGHT and `BodyView.set_facing` flips it when the enemy moves left. So anything with a direction (a head and tongue, a face that leans, a weapon) must lead on the RIGHT side of the frame, and a symmetric front view must really be centred. The first ghost had its face one pixel left of its body centre, so it looked over its shoulder as it moved, and the snakes had their heads on the left, so they slithered tail-first; both were fixed.
- **Art lessons from this sprint (the user's feedback):** compare the frames of every animation on the sheet, not only the first; look at the whole composition (a floating weapon, a flat slab of arms and shoulders, empty corners beside the neck read as awkward); fill the flat areas with shading, pads or shapes that break them up; and make the key feature of an enemy unmistakable at 1x (the zombie needed a face and ribs).
