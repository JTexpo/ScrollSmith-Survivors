## 2026-11-19: Sprint 1 stress test (desktop)

Command: `godot --path . res://scenes/world/stress_test.tscn -- --enemies=N --seconds=10` (windowed, Compatibility renderer, vsync off, Apple M1 Mac). The scene keeps N full enemies alive (chasing, active hitboxes and hurtboxes, taking fire from a rotating attacker at 20 shots/s) around a stationary invulnerable player. Frame times come from `_process` delta (wall clock), after a 30-frame warmup.

| Enemies | avg fps | avg frame ms | p99 frame ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 940.8 | 1.06 | 1.39 |
| 300 | 733.6 | 1.36 | 1.52 |

Caveats:

- The `avg_physics_ms` and `avg_process_ms` columns (Godot's `Performance` monitors) do not agree with the wall-clock frame times, so they are not used for the verdict.
- The p99 spike at 500 enemies (8.64 ms) is worth watching if the count grows.
- The enemies spawn within 40-200 px of the player and pile onto them, which is close to a worst case for overlap checks.

## 2026-11-19: Sprint 2 (base spell system)

Decisions (details in `sprint-2-design.md`):

- `SpellData` is configuration only. `SpellInstance` (plain `RefCounted`) owns current mana and the cast cooldown, so two copies of one spell never share mana. `SpellLoadout` holds five slots and the selected index, and the Player only forwards input to it.
- A depleted spell stays selected and does nothing: no auto-switch, and the "dry" cue plays once per press (a latch cleared by `end_cast()` or a selection change). The player picks the next spell on purpose, because mana never regenerates.
- `SpellData.max_hits` and `HitboxComponent.max_hits` make impact data-driven. Every Sprint 2 spell is `1`, so nothing pierces yet, but Sprint 3's piercing spells only need a bigger number. A projectile never hits the same enemy twice (`hit_cooldown` is longer than its lifetime).
- Controllers cycle spells with the shoulder buttons (`spell_prev`, `spell_next`), skipping empty slots. Keys 1-5 select directly. On touch, the `SpellBar` slot widgets handle `InputEventScreenTouch` themselves (mouse emulation is off) and ignore mouse clicks, so tapping a slot never casts. F9 (`debug_restore_mana`) refills all mana. It is handled in `Main` and marked DEBUG.
- Starting numbers (tune by playing): Fire 10 dmg / 0.35 s / cost 1 / mana 40; Thunder 7 / 0.18 / 1 / 60; Land 18 / 0.7 / 2 / 40; Sea 9 / 0.3 / 1 / 45. A full Fire pool is about 40 casts, roughly 20 kills at 2 hits per enemy.
- The stress test uses `stress_spell.tres` (Fire's values, a billion mana, 0.05 s interval), so it measures the real casting path and never runs dry.

Known limitation: damage is worked out when the spell is cast, with no defender element, because enemies have no element yet. `ElementTable` and `DamageSystem.calculate(base, attacker, defender)` are built and unit-tested. Sprint 4 should move the multiplier to the moment of impact.

Stress test after Sprint 2 (same method as Sprint 1, Apple M1, windowed, vsync off):

|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 696.6 | 1.44 | 3.61 |

Sprint 1 was 733.6 and 535.3 fps, so the spell path costs about 5%. The 60 fps target is still met with a large margin.


## 2026-11-19: Sprint 3 (spell Areas)

Decisions (details in `sprint-3-design.md`):

- Five Areas, with **Wave** added at the user's request: a wide crescent that travels outward, widens a little, and hits every enemy it passes once. Each Area is one scene behind `launch(spell, origin, direction, target)`, with defaults in an `AreaData` resource. A spell carries `range_scale` and `size_scale`, which Sprint 6 crafting will write. The rule linking range to mana cost is left to the Spell Builder (Sprint 6).
- `AreaData` is the single source of truth for a spell's Area. `SpellData.area` and `projectile_lifetime` were removed, and Missile's lifetime is now range / speed. `AreaType` moved to `AreaData` with `WAVE` appended.
- Mouse Area spells land at the cursor, clamped to range. Stick and touch land at the spell's full range in the aim direction. A small `TargetReticle` shows the spot (TEMPORARY).
- Line and Cone are instant shapes that last about 0.12 s. Cone angle is clamped to 1-359 degrees. Area zones tick on `tick_interval`. All five hit through the existing `HitboxComponent` (`hit_cooldown`), so no hitbox changes were needed.
- A spell with no Area now casts nothing and spends no mana (this fixes a Sprint 2 deferred minor).
- Starting numbers (tune by playing): Fire Missile 10 dmg / 0.35 s / cost 1 / mana 40; Thunder Cone 8 / 0.4 / 2 / 60; Land Area 12 per tick / 1.0 / 4 / 60; Sea Line 14 / 0.6 / 2 / 50; Sea Wave 12 / 0.8 / 3 / 60. Slots 1-5 hold them in that order.

Known limitations:
- Damage is still worked out at cast time through `AreaEffect._configure_hitbox`. Sprint 4 moves the elemental multiplier to impact there.
- The once-per-target guarantee and the Area tick use wall-clock time (`hit_cooldown`). Safe with no pause yet. Revisit when pause exists.
- Overlapping Area zones from repeated casts all tick, so spamming them stacks damage (paid for in mana).

## 2026-11-19: Sprint 4 (elemental enemies)

Decisions (details in `sprint-4-design.md`):

- The elemental multiplier now applies at impact. The hitbox carries the attacker's element and the hurtbox holds the defender's, so the Sprint 2 limitation (multiplier at cast time) is gone. `AreaEffect._configure_hitbox` sets raw damage and element.
- Rules, decided with the user: same element is normal damage (no same-type disadvantage). An enemy takes the strong multiplier (1.5) from the element that beats it and the weak one (0.5) from the one it beats. The README example that said a Fire enemy resists Fire was corrected to match.
- `ElementTable.Effect` (NEUTRAL, VULNERABLE, RESISTED) rides on `hit_received` as a fourth parameter, so feedback now and buffs later (Ignore Resistance, Double Vulnerability) can use it.
- Five enemies, one scene, different data: plain (grey) 20 hp / 10 dmg / speed 40, Fire 16 / 12 / 55, Thunder 12 / 8 / 75, Land 50 / 14 / 28, Sea 24 / 10 / 42. Spawn weights: plain 1, elements 2 each. `SpawnConfig.enemy_data` became `enemy_pool`.
- Damage numbers: one pooled node (64), one `_draw`, only processes while numbers are alive. Neutral white, vulnerable larger yellow with "!", resisted smaller grey. Hit flash is unchanged.
- The player has no element by default (so hits on the player are neutral), but that is only a default: the hurtbox `element` property lets equipment give the player resistances or vulnerabilities later.
- During the MCP run a shadowing warning appeared (`position` parameter on a `Node2D`), fixed by renaming it `world_position`.

Known limitations:
- Overkill hits show the full damage dealt (a 15-damage hit on a 3 health enemy shows "15"). The user is happy with this.
- Nothing in the game teaches the element cycle. Players learn from the numbers and enemy colours. A legend belongs to a later UI sprint.

## 2026-11-19: Sprint 5 (scroll collection)

Decisions (details in `sprint-5-design.md`):

- One `ScrollData` class with a category and optional category fields (the user chose this over a class per category). 15 scrolls: 4 Base, 5 Area (Wave included, since the user added it) and 6 Buff. Buff scrolls are only collectible this sprint. Rarity is data for display, and drop weights are authored per table.
- Themed drops (the user's idea, with the condition that the Base scroll is heavily preferred but never guaranteed): each enemy has its own `DropTable`. An elemental enemy weights its own Base scroll 12 and the others 1, which is 80% of its Base drops and about 45% of all its drops. Every table has a 6% drop chance. Area scrolls weigh 1-2 and Buff scrolls 0.25-1.
- Pickup is automatic by walking over the scroll (the user's call). One `PickupCollector` on the player is the only active detector, and scrolls on the ground are passive. Cap 100: a new drop removes the oldest.
- The inventory is a Player component, per run, stacking by id, with no limit. `remove` is all or nothing so crafting can never half-consume a recipe.
- Display is a toggleable panel (key `I`, controller button Y, touch button), as the user chose, plus a "+ Fire Scroll" toast. The panel does not pause the game. F10 gives 3 of every scroll (DEBUG, delete later).
- Starting numbers (tune by playing): `drop_chance` 0.06 everywhere, weights as above. Pickup radius 14 px.

Known limitations:
- The panel and toast are TEMPORARY. The panel does not pause, so enemies keep attacking while it is open.
- There is no inventory limit, no scroll art, and nothing consumes scrolls yet (Sprint 6).
- With 100 pickups on the ground, frame time rises noticeably (see below). The final review measured the cost per pickup at roughly 3-4 µs for a bob tween, 3 µs for its `Area2D` and 1 µs for its polygon, so the bob is now a shader (no tween or script per pickup). The remaining cost is mostly the `Area2D` and polygon on each pickup. Check it on a phone or the web build, where the margin is much smaller, before optimising further.

## 2026-11-19: Tap to restart did nothing on a phone

- **Found by the user on the GitHub Pages build:** after dying, "tap to restart" did nothing on a phone, while pressing R worked.
- **Cause:** the arena floor is a `ColorRect`, and Godot's `ColorRect` stops mouse and touch input by default. It covers the whole screen, so every tap was swallowed before the death screen, which listened in `_unhandled_input`, could hear it. The player and enemy bodies, the Lectern parts and the missile body had the same default.
- **Proven on a real window** with a throwaway script that kills the player and sends a genuine touch: the original code did not restart, the floor fix alone did, and both fixes together did.
- **Fix, in two layers:** every decorative `ColorRect` in the world scenes now ignores input, and the death screen listens in `_input`, which runs before any control, so a future screen-sized control cannot break restart again. A guard test fails if a world scene gets a Control that stops touches.
- **A test-environment finding:** in headless test runs, mouse and touch events never reach `_unhandled_input`, so a headless test cannot show this class of bug. The headless test therefore guards only the `_input` layer, and the root cause is guarded by the world-scene check.
- Still untested on a real phone: the other touch paths on the web build (the Interact button, the crafting screen, the inventory button). The user reports the sticks, aiming and slot taps work.


## 2026-11-20: Sprint 7 (buffs)

Decisions (details in `sprint-7-design.md`):
- All six buffs work. A buff is **data**: a `BuffEffect` resource on the Buff scroll (your choice over a script per buff). Multipliers multiply, bonuses add, and a new buff is a new `.tres`.
- **Best match** for several Bases (your vision): each hit uses whichever element does best against that enemy (strong beats neutral beats weak, the first element wins a tie). It lives only in `DamageSystem.resolve`. The strong multiplier is the only thing Double Vulnerability scales (one stack is 2.0x, two are 2.5x), and a resisted hit under Ignore Resistance becomes a normal hit.
- **Duplicate Area is a ring** (your choice): aimed Areas fire copies evenly around the player with the first on the aim, ground zones keep the first at the target and ring the rest at 1.5 x the zone radius. One cast pays mana once, and the copies are independent hits.
- **Additional Base:** each stack opens one more Base slot. The Base count must equal 1 plus the stacks, with no repeated element.
- Damage stacking is uncapped (your call: a bigger number drains the pool faster). Caps: Duplicate Area 4, Additional Base 3, Ignore Resistance 1.
- Buff numbers (per stack): Damage x1.5 (cost x1.5), Max Mana x1.5 (cost x1.25), Double Vulnerability +100% strong bonus (cost x1.25), Ignore Resistance (cost x1.25), Duplicate Area +1 shot (cost x2.0), Additional Base +1 Base (cost x1.8). Cost rises at least as fast as damage or copies, so damage per mana never improves. **Increased Max Mana is the deliberate exception:** it gives more casts, not stronger ones, so it is the one buff that makes a spell last longer.
- A spell whose cost is above its whole pool is refused (`REASON_TOO_COSTLY`). The crafting screen stays one screen: a buff row with minus and plus steppers, a multi-select Base column ("Base 2 of 2"), and a preview with the drawbacks in plain words ("Buffs: costs 2.3x per cast, 100 casts become 44.").

Migration notes: `SpellBuilder.build` takes a list of Bases and the buff scrolls, `CraftingRequest.bases` replaces `base`, `CraftingSession.bases` replaces `base`, `REASON_BUFFS_LOCKED` is gone, and `SpellLoadout.try_cast` now loops over `CastPattern.shots`. The Sprint 6 check order (Base, Area, wrong Base, wrong Area, range) is kept before the new buff checks.

Known limitations:
- Balance is untested in play. The numbers are a first guess.
- A hybrid spell is tinted with its first Base's colour.
- The crafting screen is TEMPORARY in look, and long buff names (such as Double Vulnerability Damage) are clipped in the buff row.
- Nothing teaches the player the recipe rules beyond the crafting screen's own messages.

## 2026-11-20: Sprint 9 (Gold, equipment and the shop)

Decisions:

- **Starting-spell buffs are picked or re-rolled every run and never saved (your decision, from player feedback).** The level table is unchanged: levels 7 and 17 are random rewards (re-rolled each run) and 12 and 19 are chosen ones (picked each run). `buff_picks` left the save and old files still load.
- **Scope B (your choice):** stats plus one effect each for Wands (chance to cast twice) and Staffs (chance to deal double damage). The other README effects wait.
- **Approach 1 (your choice):** the existing `StatBonuses` path plus a per-cast `damage_scale` on the effect and a cast-speed bonus on the spell instance. Nothing is written into a shared `SpellData`.
- Gold: plain, Fire and Thunder enemies give 1, Sea 2, Land 3 (my call). Coins share the gem pickup and dropper (150 cap, merge at the cap). Gold is banked as collected and saved like XP.
- Equipment: 15 items in three slots (Robe, Weapon, Headwear). Prices 80 to 350 Gold, element Robes +15% element damage, Wands trade damage for speed and Staffs the reverse (all my calls, tune by playing). ## 2026-11-22: Sprint 10 (potions and consumables)

Decisions:
- **Carry everything you own, with a potion bar (your choice, "A").** Potions are bought one at a time in the death-screen shop, the stock is saved (up to 99 of each), and unused potions survive death.
- **Approach 1 (your choice):** potions are data (`PotionData` in a `PotionCatalog`), timed bonuses ride on the `StatBonuses` path, and the Player holds three bonus layers (level, equipment, temporary) with one `refresh_bonuses`. This replaced the two places that combined level and equipment by hand.
- The six potions (my calls, tune by playing): Health heals 40% of max health (price 40, cooldown 10 s), Mana restores 50% of every spell's pool (60, 15 s), Speed +40% for 15 s (30, 30 s), Damage +50% spell damage for 15 s (50, 30 s), Invulnerability 5 s (100, 60 s), Luck +100% for 60 s (40, 90 s). Mana restores only part of a pool on purpose.
- Rules (my calls): each type has its own cooldown plus a 1 s shared cooldown, a timed potion can't be drunk again while its effect runs, Health is refused at full health, Mana when every spell is full, and nothing can be drunk while dead. A refused potion is never consumed and starts no cooldown. Timers and cooldowns freeze while paused.
- Luck weights Uncommon scrolls x(1 + luck) and Rare x(1 + 2 x luck), and never changes whether something drops. At luck 0 the roll is exactly the old one.
- Inputs: Z X C V B N for the six slots, D-pad left/right to select and up to drink on a controller, tap or click on a slot. F8 gives 3 of every potion (DEBUG, saved).

Rulings I made while building:
- `PotionBelt` gained `use_slot(index)` and `refresh()` (not in the plan), so keys and taps can drink by bar position and a debug gift can redraw the bar.
- `Player` gained a `hurtbox` accessor so `ActiveEffects` can grant the hurtbox its window.
- The shop's potion row is a 3 by 2 grid with short labels ("Health  40g  x2"), because six buttons did not fit in one row at 480 pixels.
- `ActiveEffects.clear()` leaves the hurtbox's own window to run out by itself.

Migration notes: `RunProgress.equipment_bonuses` is gone (the Player's `equipment_bonuses` layer replaces it), `DropTable.roll` takes an optional luck, `ShopScreen.open` takes an optional potion catalog, `ShopResult` has a `potion` field, `PlayerProfile` has `potions`, and `StatBonuses.Stat` has `LUCK`.

Known limitations:
- Potion numbers, prices and the luck formula are first guesses and untested in play.
- Potions are only bought in the death-screen shop, and none drop from enemies.
- A controller can't drink a potion directly (it selects with the D-pad, then presses up), and there is no confirmation dialog when buying.
- All the new HUD and shop pieces are TEMPORARY looks.
- The refusal "Still cooling down." shows for a timed potion drunk again during its effect, because the type cooldown outranks "effect running" in the checking order.

Findings during the build:
- Touches never reach buttons because mouse emulation is off, so the potion slots handle their own touches. A real-window script (3x scale, a throwaway profile) confirmed a tap on a slot drinks exactly once and fires no spell, and a second tap during the cooldown is refused.
- A real-window screenshot of the shop showed the new Potions row and the detail text sit under the equipment columns without overlapping.
- Stress with `--drops` (300 enemies, windowed, median of three, 0.05 s interval): Missile 475.1, Wave 103.6, Zone 175.9 fps, unchanged from Sprint 9 (469.7, 105.4, 173.2). The new components cost nothing measurable.
